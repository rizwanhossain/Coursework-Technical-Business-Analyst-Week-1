# Discovery Brief

## Problem summary

Legacy Trust Bank faces significant challenges managing delinquent accounts due to mismatched legacy data management systems, spreadsheets, emails, and makeshift workarounds. Currently, there are over 100,000 deliquent accounts being managed by a team of 50+ representatives.

Common workarounds include:
- spreadsheet trackers
- email templates

Other methods rely on individual knowledge and manual discrepancy fixes. As a result, duplicate work is being completed and follow-ups on deliquent accounts are not being properly monitored. Customers have been forced to contact the bank, often having long, repetitive conversations to get to the root of the problem.

The finance and compliance director, Daniel Okoye, predicts these delays and inconsistencies are resulting a revenue loss of 15%.

As the number of deliquent accounts increases, these fixes are proving to be ineffective as they lack consistency, efficiency, and accuracy.

Potential stakeholder tensions:
- Gareth and Amina may differ on their definitions of "straightforward cases"
- Daniel biases towards financial savings, while Amina's are toward operational savings

## Discovery questions

- Where is duplicate information stored across different formats?
- Which customer groups would be most receptivet to self-service and digital bank services?
- Which figures regarding cost and wastage are reliable enough to represent the current process?
- Which improvement would have the most impact on the current process, by itself?
- Where are stakeholders likely to be most resistant to change?
- Which steps in debt recovery are high-volume and rules-driven enough for self-service?
- Where do spreadsheets and manual handoffs create duplicate work?
- Which baseline metrics best show operational waste and revenue leakage?

## Final problem statement

Representatives waste  time managing duplicated contact attempts, manual data consolidation across fragmented systems, and inconsistent follow-ups—preventing the bank from contacting customers at the right time and resulting in an estimated 15% revenue loss on recoverable accounts.

**Root causes**
- Data fragmentation
- Inconsistent process standards
- Weak follow-up accountability
- Manual data entry

With 100,000+ accounts and 50+ representatives, the system cannot scale. Straightforward cases are delayed, customers re-contact multiple times, and the best representatives have already learned workarounds that make them allegedly faster than the official process—meaning the process itself is fundamentally broken.
